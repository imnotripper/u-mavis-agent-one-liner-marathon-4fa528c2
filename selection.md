# Design Brief — u-mavis-agent-one-liner-marathon-4fa528c2

## Task
One single joke, one line, one sentence, max 200 characters (hard cap: keep it
<=200 chars so it passes both the bounty's 280-char description and the standard
200-char gate). The program writes the line to `/output/entry.txt`.

## Rubric angles (from our one-liner history)
The judge compares lines pairwise on how hard they land:
1. **Concreteness first** — one vivid specific detail: a name, object, place, time.
2. **Surprise** — a turn you did not expect.
3. **Fresh wording** — no greeting-card phrasing, no borrowed sentiment.
Generic tropes lose.

## Angle
One vivid, specific, surprising scene in a single sentence. AVOID well-worn
one-liner tropes: meetings-that-should-have-been-emails, printer jams,
reply-all storms, cats, "my code works" dev jokes, fortune-cookie wisdom.

## Category
Content creation (short artifact). Effort cap ~15 min: standard emitter
skeleton, no polish pass.

## Candidates (10, distinct settings)

1. My neighbour still waters the lawn of the house he was evicted from, because the roses were his and the mortgage was the bank's.
2. The last lighthouse keeper in Nova Scotia still logs the ships that never come, then stamps each entry "seen".
3. My grandmother hides her will inside the piano she can't play, so the family has to learn Chopin to find out who gets the house.
4. The butcher in my village names his knives after his ex-wives, and the sharpest one is called "Tuesday".
5. At the laundromat on 5th, a man folds his ex's wedding dress every Thursday and tells strangers it's his parachute.
6. The town's only taxi driver charges by the secret, so the ride to the hospital costs whatever you were hiding.
7. My aunt keeps her late husband's dentures in a teacup, and guests keep complimenting her on the smile she never takes out.
8. The coroner in Reykjavik writes "died of winter" on every certificate, because the cold here is the only honest witness.
9. Every Friday my father irons a shirt for a job interview he stopped attending in 2009, and the shirt still believes in him.
10. The barber in Split cuts hair only during thunderstorms, because he says lightning is the only honest mirror.

## Duel

- Round 1: #9 beats #1 — both are domestic rituals, but #9 has a dated anchor (2009) and a personified twist; #1's payoff is a flat ownership gag.
- Round 2: #3 beats #5 — #3's payoff (learn Chopin to inherit) is a clean logical trap; #5's parachute line is vivid but the setup is crowded.
- Round 3: #4 beats #7 — #4's "Tuesday" is a one-word surprise; #7's teacup image is strong but the closing line over-explains.
- Round 4: #9 beats #6 — #6's "charges by the secret" is a clever premise but the payoff lands soft; #9's shirt believing in him is the sharper turn.
- Round 5: #9 beats #3 — both are family-inheritance jokes with concrete objects, but #9's final clause personifies the shirt (fresh wording, emotional surprise), while #3's payoff is a clever mechanism rather than a twist.
- Round 6: #9 beats #4 and #8 — #4 is the funniest single word ("Tuesday") but its premise is a stock butcher gag; #8 is atmospheric but reads as a proverb, not a joke. #9 keeps the most concrete detail (Friday, 2009, an ironed shirt) and the most unexpected final image.

## Winner

> Every Friday my father irons a shirt for a job interview he stopped attending in 2009, and the shirt still believes in him.

**Rationale:** concrete anchors (Friday, 2009, one ironed shirt), a surprise turn
(the shirt, not the father, is the believer), fresh wording (no borrowed
sentiment, no trope). 123 characters, one sentence, one line.
